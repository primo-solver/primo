## Section 2: the instantiation loop

The ground solver abstracts "all atoms and quantified formulas and treat[s] them
as if they were propositional variables". It returns an assignment `E ∪ Q`: `E`
is a set of ground literals and `Q` a set of quantified formulas that together
propositionally entail the input.

The instantiation module adds lemmas `∀x̄. ϕ ⇒ ϕσ`, where `σ` maps the variables of
`ϕ` to ground terms. The loop ends when the added instances make the ground problem
unsatisfiable.

The paper assumes every quantified formula is `∀x̄. ϕ` with `ϕ` quantifier-free,
"achieved by prenex form transformation and Skolemization".

## Section 3: which terms suffice

- **Theorem 1.** `E ∪ Q` (ground literals and universally quantified clauses,
  with at least one term of each sort) is unsatisfiable iff there is a series of
  finite instance sets `Qi` with `E ∪ Q1 ∪ … ∪ Qn` unsatisfiable, where each
  `Qi+1` uses only substitutions with `ran(σ) ⊆ T(E ∪ Q1 ∪ … ∪ Qi)`. Only terms
  already present, or generated by earlier instances, are needed, not the whole
  Herbrand universe.
- **Scope of the theorem.** It holds in pure first-order logic with equality, and
  in theories that preserve compactness. For EPR formulas the term sets are
  finite, so the enumeration always terminates.
- **Lemma 1.** An infinite series of satisfiable ground assignments `Ei`, where
  `Qi` instantiates `Q` with terms from `T(Ei)` and `Ei+1 |= Ei ∪ Qi`, implies
  that `E ∪ Q` is satisfiable.
- **Corollary 1 (model soundness).** If the module cannot generate an instance
  that `E` does not already entail, using terms `T(E)`, the input is satisfiable.
- **Corollary 2 (refutational completeness).** On an unsatisfiable input, the
  loop terminates provided instance generation is fair.

## Section 4 and Figure 2: strategy u

Definition 1: an instantiation strategy maps a `T`-satisfiable set of ground
literals `E` and a quantified formula `∀x̄. ϕ` to a set of substitutions with
domain `x̄`.

Figure 2 lists four strategies: conflict-based (c), E-matching (e), model-based
(m) and enumerative (u).

Enumerative instantiation `u(E, ∀x̄. ϕ)`:

1. choose an ordering `≺` on tuples of quantifier-free terms;
2. return `{x̄ ↦ t̄}`, where `t̄` is a minimal tuple from `T(E)` such that
   `E ⊭ ϕ{x̄ ↦ t̄}`, or `∅` if none exists.

The paper's comparison with model-based instantiation: u considers every
substitution whose instance `E` does not entail, and introduces predicates on
fewer new terms ("minimally diverse"). Model-based instantiation suits
satisfiable problems better (Example 3).

## Section 4.1: implementation details in CVC4

- **Tuple ordering.** `(t1…tn) ≺ (s1…sn)` if `max ti ≺ max si`, or the maxima
  are equal and the tuples are lexicographically ordered. Tuples involving a
  later term come only after every combination of earlier terms. The underlying
  term order is dynamic: a finite list `t1 ≺ … ≺ tn` grows by a term that `E`
  does not entail equal to any `ti`, once all combinations are entailed. "The
  particular choice of t beyond this criteria is arbitrary."
- **Entailment checks.** A cache of already returned instances, plus an
  incomplete fast check. It uses congruence classes over `T(E)`, a per-function
  term index, and iterated rewriting of a literal to `⊤`, extended to Boolean
  structure.
- **Restricting the enumeration space.** Relevant domains (Ge and de Moura), or
  sort inference, shrink the candidate terms.
- **Compound strategies.** Priority `s1; s2` and interleaving `s1 + s2`. The
  paper reports `c;e;u` and `c;e+u` as most effective for unsatisfiable
  problems.

## Implemented subset and deviations

- **Strategy.** Strategy u alone. No c or e, and so no compound strategy.
- **Skolemization.** No prenex or Skolem-normal-form preprocessing. A quantifier
  atom assigned so that it needs Skolemization (a true `∃`, or a false `∀`) gets
  one Skolem lemma with fresh constants. Nested quantifiers in an instance become
  new quantifier atoms of the lemma, handled the same way.
- **Term pool.** Ground subterms of the input assertions and of every added
  lemma, a superset of `T(E)`. Theorem 1's term set `T(E ∪ Q1 ∪ … ∪ Qi)` justifies
  this for refutation. Lemma 1 and Corollary 1 are stated for `T(Ei)`, so no
  model-soundness claim is made from this pool.
- **Term order.** Order of first appearance, which the paper allows ("arbitrary").
  Entailed-equal terms are not merged.
- **Entailment.** The instance cache only; no congruence-based entailment check.
  More instances may be generated, but each is still a valid consequence.
- **Answers.** `unsat` from the ground solver. `sat` only when every assigned
  quantifier atom is covered by a Skolem lemma, so that no universal remains.
  Otherwise `unknown`: without the entailment check, Corollary 1 is not
  available.
- **Empty sorts and Bool.** Theorem 1 assumes a term of every sort. A sort with
  no ground term gets one fresh constant. Bool-sorted variables range over
  `true` and `false` only.
- **Rounds.** At each complete assignment, at most
  `--quantifier-instances-per-round` new instances are queued, shared evenly
  across the universally behaving atoms, starting from a rotating one. One solve
  tries at most `--quantifier-max-instances` instances, then answers `unknown`
  with reason `resource-limit`.
- **Not implemented.** Relevant domains, sort inference, and model export.

## Tests derived from the paper

- Example 1 and Example 2 shapes: the instance on the minimal term refutes.
- Example 3 shape: repeated enumeration over three terms ends in `unknown`, not
  `sat`.
- Fairness of the tuple order: a term appended later is used only after all
  combinations of earlier terms.
- Theorem 1: a refutation needing a term that appears only in an earlier
  instance.
